Practical guides to data that is wrong without anything failing: what goes wrong in production warehouses, and the SQL that catches it. One new guide each weekday in the 30 Days of Data Reliability series.

  • Day 1 of 30 Days of Data Reliability Data that is wrong without anything failingThe costliest data problems in a warehouse don't raise an error. Here are the five that show up most often, what each looks like, and one SQL check that catches it.
  • Day 2 of 30 Days of Data Reliability Freshness: the one check every table needsIf a table gets only one check, make it freshness. It is one line of SQL, but three details decide whether it catches a stopped load or quietly passes.
  • Day 3 of 30 Days of Data Reliability Volume: on time, but half the rowsA table can be fresh and still be wrong: the load ran on time but brought half the rows, or ran twice. A volume check catches both, if you compare against the right baseline.
  • Day 4 of 30 Days of Data Reliability Schema drift: someone renamed a column upstreamAn upstream team renames a column and their deploy goes fine. Yours often does too, and that is the problem: the column quietly becomes NULL and every downstream number shifts.
  • Day 5 of 30 Days of Data Reliability Why your dbt tests miss thingsIf you use dbt, you have already written good data quality rules. The catch is when they run: only when dbt does. When a load stalls, dbt has nothing to run, and every test stays green.
